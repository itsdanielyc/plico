# Set up a phone inbox

1. Create a folder for received scans, such as `Documents/Phone Inbox`. Give it a different path from your scanner output and the library's managed folders.
2. In Plico's Library sidebar, choose **Phone inbox… → Choose phone folder…** and select that folder.
3. Scan on your phone and send the PDF or image to the folder. With Plico open, the file enters the Inbox after the transfer settles. The original stays in the receiving folder. An empty file can receive its contents later and Plico will pick it up.

## Send scans from your phone

- **Continuity Camera on macOS.** In Finder, open your phone inbox folder. Control-click in the folder and choose the iPhone or iPad's **Scan Documents** action. Scan and save on the phone. The PDF appears in Finder. [Apple Continuity Camera guide](https://support.apple.com/102332)
- **LocalSend on Windows, macOS, or Linux.** Set the desktop app's download folder to the phone inbox. On your phone, share the scan to LocalSend and select your computer on the same network. Accept the transfer in LocalSend. [LocalSend](https://localsend.org/)
- **KDE Connect on Linux.** Pair your phone and desktop. Open the desktop settings for that device and set the receiving folder in the **Share and receive** plugin. Share the scan to the desktop through KDE Connect. [KDE Connect handbook](https://docs.kde.org/stable5/en/kdeconnect-kde/kdeconnect-kde/index.html)
- **A synced or shared folder.** Select its local desktop copy in Plico and save scans into the top level from your phone. Configure your transfer app to download full files to the desktop.

Use **Import existing files** to check the current folder or **Resume and catch up** to process transfers delivered while Plico was closed. Enable **Catch up on open** in the phone inbox menu to do this automatically for the current library. **Stop phone inbox** pauses that watch for the current session. The scanner watch has its own controls.

If a document stays missing, check the sender's destination and confirm the received file has contents. Saving as PDF is the simplest option when the phone's default image format is unsupported. The library's **Include watched scans** setting applies to both sources, including retries.
